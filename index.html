Methodology
CS109 Concepts Applied
01
Monte Carlo Simulation
1,000 independent future Earths sampled from 8 variable distributions. Each particle is one draw from the joint probability space.
xi(k) ~ Fi(μi, σi),   k = 1…1000
02
Distribution Families
Three families model different uncertainty types: Normal (symmetric, measurable), Uniform (genuinely unknown), Bernoulli (binary event with known probability).
Normal · Uniform · Bernoulli(p)
03
Maximum Likelihood (MLE)
Parameters fit from real datasets. Methane: 30 country-year observations. Food waste: Project Drawdown impact range. Tipping points: IPCC AR6 probability interval.
μ̂ = (1/n) Σ xi  ·  σ̂² = (1/n) Σ (xi−μ̂)²
04
Bayesian Conditioning
Selecting a variable reveals its value at μ ± 1.2σ. Particles outside this window are eliminated — the posterior collapses around the revealed information.
Sj = { x : |xj − μj| ≤ 1.2σj }
05
Shannon Entropy
Measures how spread out the warming outcome distribution is. Computed over 10 temperature bins from 1.5°C–4.0°C. Maximum entropy = maximum uncertainty.
H = −Σ pi log2(pi)
06
Information Gain
How much does one question reduce uncertainty? Ranked by 2·w·σ — the product of climate impact weight and distributional spread. Tipping points rank 15× above solar.
ΔH = Hbefore − Hafter  ·  I = 2·w·σ
